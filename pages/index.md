### Welcome, fellow pilgrim!

Whether you're traveling for contemplation, worship, healing or the thrill of a new encounter, you've come to the right place.

Popular concepts of the Middle Ages—think Game of Thrones—draw on limited, mostly European, models of the world between 500 and 1500. The [Global Middle Ages movement](http://globalmiddleages.org/about), on which there is [substantial scholarship](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/abs/global-middle-ages/8A93324E51AC2137A659ACD266A9165C), expands our idea of the Middle Ages to celebrate the breadth of cultural, religious and social interchange that characterized the entire world between 500 and 1500.

*   **Explore the Meaning of Pilgrimage:** Experience aspects of a pilgrimage and reflect on its significance as a deeply human, embodied activity!
    
*   **Understand History’s Context:** Investigate how people's views of the history they experience are shaped by their context (the social, cultural, economic, and political norms that they don't control), and how how modern assumptions can influence our understanding of that historical context.
    
*   **Develop Critical Historical Skills:** Carefully evaluate primary historical sources written/created/produced by people in the past, analyze those sources in their historical context to look for missing information, and actively seek out new perspectives in the secondary sources produced by modern historians about primary sources to enrich your understanding of the past.
    
*   **Challenge Traditional Assumptions:** Use modern tools to question outdated ideas about what actually was "medieval", which parts of what we think of as "medieval" were created by early-modern and Victorian scholars, and understand the difference applications of "medieval" in both its historical context and in the modern world.
